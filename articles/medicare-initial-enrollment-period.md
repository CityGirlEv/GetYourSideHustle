Signing up for Medicare when you turn 65 is governed by a strict timeline called the **Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)**. This is a 7-month window that centers on your 65th birthday month. Because the federal government enforces permanent lifetime premium surcharges if you miss this window, knowing how to count your seven months is the most critical starting step for your transition.

This guide provides a practical, step-by-step walkthrough to help you calculate your exact IEP dates, determine your coverage start date, and manage the timing rules.

Before diving into calculations, review the general milestones in our core guide to medicare enrollment periods. If you are preparing a detailed checklist, check out our guide on 3 months before 65: your medicare action plan.

## 1. The Standard 7-Month IEP Formula

For the vast majority of people, the Initial Enrollment Period starts exactly three months before the month you turn 65, spans your birthday month, and ends exactly three months after your birthday month.

### Standard Calculation Example (Birthday: October 15)
If your 65th birthday is October 15, your birthday month is October. Here is how your 7-month window is counted:

* **Month 1 (3 Months Before):** July
* **Month 2 (2 Months Before):** August
* **Month 3 (1 Month Before):** September
* **Month 4 (Birthday Month):** October
* **Month 5 (1 Month After):** November
* **Month 6 (2 Months After):** December
* **Month 7 (3 Months After):** January

Your IEP opens on **July 1** and closes on **January 31**. To ensure your coverage starts on October 1 (your birthday month), you must submit your application in July, August, or September.

## 2. The 1st-of-the-Month Birthday Exception (The Shift Rule)

There is a critical exception to the standard IEP formula. Under CMS rules, if your birthday falls on the **first day of the month**, your entire Medicare timeline is shifted forward by one full month.

### Shifted Calculation Example (Birthday: October 1)
If your 65th birthday is October 1, the government treats you as if you turned 65 in September. Your birthday month for Medicare purposes is September. Here is how your 7-month window is counted:

* **Month 1 (3 Months Before):** June
* **Month 2 (2 Months Before):** July
* **Month 3 (1 Month Before):** August
* **Month 4 (Medicare Birthday Month):** September
* **Month 5 (1 Month After):** October
* **Month 6 (2 Months After):** November
* **Month 7 (3 Months After):** December

Your IEP opens on **June 1** and closes on **December 31**. Your coverage starts on **September 1**. If you wait until October to apply, your coverage will not start until November 1, leaving you with a coverage gap.

---

## 3. How Application Month Dictates Your Start Date

When your coverage becomes active depends entirely on which month of your IEP you submit your online application:

* **Apply in Months 1, 2, or 3 (Before Birthday Month):** Your coverage begins on the **1st day of your birthday month**.
* **Apply in Month 4 (Birthday Month):** Your coverage begins on the **1st day of the following month**.
* **Apply in Months 5, 6, or 7 (After Birthday Month):** Your coverage begins on the **1st day of the month after you apply**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What happens if I miss the last day of my IEP?
If you miss the 7-month deadline, you are locked out of enrolling in Medicare until the next General Enrollment Period (GEP), which runs from January 1 to March 31 of each year. Your coverage will be delayed, and you may face a permanent late enrollment penalty.

### Do I get a separate IEP for prescription drug coverage (Part D)?
Your IEP for Part D is the same 7-month window as your IEP for Part A and Part B. You must enroll in a drug plan during this time to avoid the Part D late enrollment penalty.
