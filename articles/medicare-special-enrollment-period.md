If you delayed enrolling in Medicare when you turned 65 because you were covered under an active employer group health plan, you cannot simply sign up whenever you want. You must wait for a specific window called a **Special Enrollment Period (SEP)**. SEPs are triggered by qualifying life events and allow you to enroll in Medicare Part A and Part B without facing late enrollment penalties.

Understanding the timing and documentation requirements for an SEP is essential to avoid gaps in coverage and secure your health insurance during retirement.

## 1. The Most Common SEP: Transitioning Off Employer Coverage

The most frequent Special Enrollment Period is triggered when you leave your job, or your active employer group health coverage ends (whichever happens first).

### Who Qualifies?
To qualify for this SEP, you must meet two conditions:
1. You are **currently covered** under a group health plan based on your own current employment or your spouse's current employment.
2. You have had **continuous group coverage** since you first became eligible for Medicare at age 65.

### The Timing Limits (Don't Miss the Windows)
* **Part A & B enrollment window:** You have an **8-month window** to sign up for Medicare Part A and Part B. This window begins the month after your employment ends or your group coverage terminates (whichever occurs first).
* **Part D (Prescription) enrollment window:** You only have a **63-day window** to sign up for a Part D drug plan. If you go more than 63 days without creditable drug coverage, you will face permanent Part D penalties.

* **Crucial Timing Tip:** Although you have 8 months to enroll in Part B, you should sign up during the month before your employer coverage ends to ensure a seamless transition without a gap in care.

## 2. Forms Required for the Employment SEP

To submit a Medicare application under a Special Enrollment Period, you cannot simply apply online without proof. You must submit two specific CMS forms to Social Security:

1. **Form CMS-40B (Application for Part B):** This is your personal application to enroll in medical insurance.
2. **Form CMS-L564 (Employment Information):** This form must be completed and signed by your employer's HR department. It verifies that you had active, continuous group health insurance through a company with 20 or more employees since age 65.

You must submit these forms together online, via mail, or in person at your local Social Security office to establish your SEP status and waive late fees.

## 3. Other Common Special Enrollment Periods

While employer transitions are the most frequent, other life events can trigger a Special Enrollment Period:

* **Relocating:** If you move to a new address outside your current plan's service area, you qualify for an SEP to switch plans.
* **Loss of Medicaid:** If you lose eligibility for state Medicaid support, you qualify for an SEP to secure Medicare coverage.
* **Plan Contract Violations:** If your insurance carrier violates its contract or misrepresents plan parameters, CMS may grant an SEP to let you change plans.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Does COBRA qualify me for a Special Enrollment Period?
No. Leaving a job and taking COBRA does not protect you. COBRA is not considered active employer coverage. Your 8-month Part B signup window begins the month after your active employment ends, regardless of whether you keep COBRA.

### What if I cannot get Form CMS-L564 signed because my former employer went out of business?
If your former employer has closed down, Social Security will accept alternative proof of continuous group health coverage, such as old W-2 forms, tax returns showing health insurance premium deductions, or monthly premium payment statements.
