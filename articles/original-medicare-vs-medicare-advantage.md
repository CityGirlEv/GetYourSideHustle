## Two different ways to receive Medicare benefits

**Original Medicare** is the federal fee-for-service program (Part A hospital and Part B medical). Many people add a separate Part D drug plan and optional Medigap supplemental coverage.

**Medicare Advantage (Part C)** is an alternative way to receive Medicare benefits through private plans approved by Medicare. These plans must cover at least what Original Medicare covers, but cost-sharing, networks, and drug coverage rules can differ.

## Questions to compare on your own

- Do you prefer predictable monthly premiums with Medigap, or a bundled MA plan with network rules?
- Are your preferred doctors and hospitals in-network for plans available in your area?
- How do your regular prescriptions appear on each plan's formulary?

## Important reminder

This article is for educational comparison only. It is not a complete listing of plans in your area. Verify details with Medicare.gov, 1-800-MEDICARE, or a licensed professional before enrolling.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this article enrollment advice?
No. Learning Center articles are educational only. They do not recommend specific plans, carriers, or enrollment actions.

### Can I have both Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage at the same time?
No. You generally choose one path for how you receive your Medicare benefits in a given period — Original Medicare (often with separate Part D and Medigap) or a Medicare Advantage plan.

### Where should I verify official Medicare rules?
Use [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov), 1-800-MEDICARE, or your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) for rules that apply to you.
